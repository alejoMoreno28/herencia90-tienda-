# Identify Football Jerseys

Identify the exact reference before writing any selling copy. A text search alone is not enough for a shirt image.

## Required Workflow

1. Inspect the image: club crest, manufacturer mark, sponsor, collar, sleeve trim, fabric pattern, badges, and back/neck details. Crop or request a closer image when those cues are obscured.
2. Run **Google Lens** first for visual reverse image matching. Use its likely match only as a lead, never as final proof.
3. Search the candidate with the club, season, and visible details. Confirm against a primary source such as the club, federation, manufacturer, or official store.
4. Obtain **two independent confirmations**: one primary source plus a reliable kit archive or reputable football publication. Match at least three visible details to the supplied photo.
5. For artist collaborations, anniversary editions, releases after a tournament, or retro reissues, verify the edition name and the actual release season separately.
6. **Do not publish a season**, type, collaboration, or historical claim when sources conflict or the visual details do not match. Mark it `Needs confirmation` and ask for a clearer front, back, neck tag, or product link.

## Non-Negotiables

- Do not infer a year from the sponsor alone.
- Do not call a shirt "official," "original," or "match-worn" unless the user confirms it.
- Do not treat a retailer listing, social post, or a replica listing as proof.
- Do not reuse an old identification just because colors look similar.
- If Google Lens is unavailable, use image search and state that visual matching was limited before finalizing.
